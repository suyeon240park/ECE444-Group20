## Stakeholders + Evidence

### Primary Users — Consumers

| Stakeholder | Needs | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday consumers, including those managing a diet-related health condition (e.g., sodium-restricted) or a food allergy | Translate ingredient/nutrition info into plain language and flag what matters for their situation, not just generic facts | 83% of US consumers read labels before buying but only 16% find health claims very trustworthy, and 1 in 5 struggle to interpret nutrition info ([NSF, 2024](https://www.nsf.org/news/americans-demand-greater-clarity-standardization-food-labeling)). Among adults with hypertension specifically, 86% still exceed the sodium limit relevant to them ([CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm6452a1.htm)) — current labels aren't closing that gap. Food allergy affects over 9% of US adults ([JAMA Network Open–based national survey](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6324316/)), and a national FDA-based survey found technical ingredient wording a serious obstacle for this group ([PubMed/AAAAI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17451802/)). |

### Secondary / Affected Stakeholders

**1. Food manufacturers and brands** — positively or negatively affected depending on how their products score/are explained.

- **Need:** Products represented fairly and accurately; avoid being penalized for ambiguous or mischaracterized data.
- **Evidence this is a real effect, not a hypothetical:** [Yuka](https://yuka.io/en/), a comparable independent food-scanning app, has driven measurable manufacturer behavior — in France, [78% of food manufacturers now factor its scores into reformulation decisions](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251209219183/en/78-of-French-Food-Manufacturers-Now-Factor-Yuka-Scores-Into-Product-Formulation), and [Intermarché reformulated over 1,000 products](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-business/yuka-food-scanner-app-user-reviews/) to improve scores. The effect isn't only positive for brands, either: [Goya sued Yuka in 2025](https://couponsinthenews.com/2025/06/04/brand-sues-over-food-scanning-apps-negative-product-reviews/) over its "high risk" ingredient callouts, alleging reputational and sales harm from what it called inaccurate characterizations. This is directly relevant to our project's credibility and accuracy requirements — if we misrepresent an ingredient's risk, there's a real reputational/legal dimension, not just a UX one.

**2. Public health and regulatory bodies** (e.g., FDA, dietary guideline authorities)

- **Need:** Consumer-facing tools that align with, rather than contradict or oversimplify, official dietary guidance.
- **Evidence:** The [FDA's own January 2025 proposed rule](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-labeling-nutrition/front-package-nutrition-labeling) requiring front-of-package labels for sodium, saturated fat, and added sugar was explicitly motivated by the same problem we're solving — the FDA states its goal is to "provide consumers, including those who have lower nutrition knowledge, with interpretive nutrition information" so they can "quickly and easily identify how foods can be part of a healthy diet." This tells us two things: (1) there's institutional validation that our core problem is real and currently unsolved at the regulatory level, and (2) our app should be designed to stay consistent with whatever FDA/Health Canada nutrient thresholds are current, since a regulator is a stakeholder in not having their guidance misrepresented downstream.
